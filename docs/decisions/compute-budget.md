# Compute budget: dialogues x turns x tok/s = wall-clock days

Written 2026-08-25 from measurements on the box, not spec sheets. Updated
2026-09-08 with the Olmo-3-7B re-measurement. Re-estimated 2026-09-28 with the
seeker half, the 300-token cap and judge time (the last section). Source: Notion task "Write the
compute budget". The live numbers and the serving recipe are in
`models/RUN_APPROACH.md`; this file records the reasoning.

## The operating point

llama.cpp Vulkan, `-np 8`, q8_0 KV cache, `--cache-reuse 256`,
`-c = (depth + 1024) * slots`.

Measured on qwen3.6:35b-a3b (`models/parallel_scaling.csv`): **116.4 tok/s
aggregate at 32k depth**, which is 2.29x the naive single-stream default
(50.9 tok/s at np=1, f16 KV). np=16 is slower than np=8 (94.0 tok/s), so do
not over-subscribe.

## Cost per arm

**Stale since 2026-09-08 (flagged 2026-09-11):** the "both dyad roles" assumption
below predates the fixed-seeker decision. The seeker's half of every arm's
tokens now runs on the seeker model, so the true figure per arm is (mentor half
/ arm tok/s) + (seeker half / seeker tok/s). Recompute once the pilot has
chosen and measured the seeker; see `docs/decisions/factorial.md`. The last
section does this as a function of the seeker's tok/s, until the pilot has it.

Assumptions: 2,700 dialogues per arm, 20 turns, 200 tokens per turn, both dyad
roles generated by the arm's model. That is 21.6 M generated tokens per arm,
so days = 21.6e6 / (aggregate tok/s) / 86,400 = 250 / tok/s.

| Arm | tok/s at 32k, np=8, q8_0 | Days per arm | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| qwen3.6:35b-a3b | 116.4 | 2.15 | measured 2026-08-25 |
| gpt-oss:20b | 83.4 | 3.0 | **measured 2026-09-08** on the HIP build (no Vulkan path for MXFP4); was ~98 / ~2.6 extrapolated |
| glm-4.7-flash | 29.1 | 8.6 | **measured 2026-09-08** on the HIP build (Vulkan 25.9); was ~60 / ~4.5 extrapolated. Weakest arm; prefill ~250 tok/s |
| Olmo-3-7B-Instruct | 41.5 | 6.0 | **measured 2026-09-08** (was ~41 / ~6.3 days extrapolated from 18.1 x 2.29; single-stream is faster than assumed, parallel factor only 1.47x, the two cancel) |

The 2026-08-25 four-arm total was about 15.6 days against 21 allocated to the
waves (W7 to W9). At the recommended 40-turn dialogues it was about 26 days,
still inside the window once the pilot and baseline are excluded.

## Sensitivity

| Turns per dialogue | Days per arm (qwen3.6) | Four-arm total (2026-08-25 basis) |
|---|---|---|
| 20 (original plan) | 2.15 | ~15.6 |
| 40 (**chosen 2026-09-10**) | 4.3 | ~26 |
| 60 | 6.4 | ~36 |

**40 turns is decided** (`docs/decisions/persona-stability.md`), and on the
measured 2026-09-08 basis it does not fit with four arms: ~39.5 days against
the 21 allocated to W7-W9. Dropping glm-4.7-flash brings it to ~22.3. The turn
count and the arm count are therefore one decision, not two.

## Caveats

- All four arms are measured at the operating point as of 2026-09-08. The
  four-arm total is about 19.8 days at 20 turns (fits the 21-day wave window)
  and about 40 at 40 turns (does not; dropping glm-4.7-flash gives about 22).
  Parallel factors at np=8: qwen3.6 2.29x, gpt-oss 1.6x, Olmo 1.47x, glm
  1.29x. The ollama blobs for gpt-oss and glm do not load in upstream
  llama.cpp; the ggml-org GGUFs do. gpt-oss needs the HIP build.
- The single largest lever is **KV cache reuse**: a turn at 32k prefills a
  handful of tokens, not 32,768. If the harness loses it, every number here
  is wrong by about three orders of magnitude. Log `prompt_n` on every call.
- Olmo-3 uses sliding-window attention; llama.cpp disables `--cache-reuse`
  (chunk-level reuse) for it but exact-prefix caching still works. Verify in
  the pilot that the reinforced-persona reminder (appended after the history)
  does not force a full re-prefill on the seeker side.

## Re-estimate, 2026-09-28: the 300-token cap, the seeker half, judge time

Added after the 2026-09-28 audits (gap audit F7 and F15). It changes no
decision above: 40 turns, three arms and the frozen grid stand. **Every number
in this section is an estimate** from the measured tok/s in
`models/RUN_APPROACH.md` and assumed turn lengths and judge latencies; the
pilot measures each input, and its numbers replace these.

Basis: the frozen grid, 2,970 dialogues per arm at 40 turns, and the fixed
seeker, so each arm's mentor half runs on the arm's model and its seeker half
on the seeker (`docs/decisions/factorial.md`). One half is 2,970 x 40 messages.
The earlier figures assume 200 tokens a message; `n_predict` is 300, so a
mentor or seeker that runs to the cap costs 1.5 times as much. The two are the
bounds; the pilot's mean `predicted_n` lands between them.

**Mentor half, per arm** (2,970 x 40 x tokens / arm tok/s / 86,400):

| Arm | tok/s | Days at 200 tokens | Days at 300 tokens |
|---|---|---|---|
| qwen3.6:35b-a3b | 116.4 | 2.4 | 3.5 |
| gpt-oss:20b (HIP) | 83.4 | 3.3 | 5.0 |
| Olmo-3-7B-Instruct | 41.5 | 6.6 | 9.9 |
| **Three arms** | | **12.3** | **18.4** |

**Seeker half**, a function of the seeker's aggregate tok/s at the operating
point, which is unknown until the pilot chooses and measures the seeker:

| Seeker tok/s | Per arm, 200 tokens | Per arm, 300 tokens | Three arms, 200 | Three arms, 300 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 9.2 | 13.8 | 27.5 | 41.3 |
| 60 | 4.6 | 6.9 | 13.8 | 20.6 |
| 100 | 2.8 | 4.1 | 8.3 | 12.4 |

**Dialogue total, three arms**, mentor plus seeker halves:

| Seeker tok/s | 200 tokens | 300 tokens |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | 39.8 | 59.7 |
| 60 | 26.0 | 39.1 |
| 100 | 20.5 | 30.8 |

The 200-token column reproduces the factorial record's "about 20.5 days at
~100 tok/s, about 40 at ~30". At the cap, only a seeker near 100 tok/s keeps
the waves near the 21 allocated to W7-W9 plus the W6 and W14 slack.

**Judge time.** The pre-analysis plan scores 30 judge calls per treated dyad
(`docs/pap/pre-analysis-plan.md` section 5: `main`, 20 seeker calls on turns
4, 8, ..., 40; `stance`, 10 mentor calls on the same turns). Control dyads get
none in either scope. Per arm that is 2,700 x 30 = 81,000 calls. `score` now
runs one dyad at a time on each judge slot, `concurrency` slots at once
(remediation W0 item 7, commit `3140d1d`), so at 8 judge slots:

| Seconds per call | Per arm | Three arms | For comparison: one slot, per arm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.6 | 1.8 | 4.7 |
| 10 | 1.2 | 3.5 | 9.4 |

The seconds per call are assumptions: a `line_to_line` prompt carries every
earlier seeker line (about 10k to 12k tokens by turn 40; gap audit F7,
parallelism review H2), and the per-dyad
ordering lets each call prefill only what the previous one on that slot did
not. The second judge's `stance` subsample (f = 0.10 proposed, 270 dyads per
arm, 10 calls each) adds about 0.02 to 0.04 days per arm. Scoring can run
beside a wave on its own lock, but on the same GPU it takes throughput from the
dialogues, so count it as machine time either way.

**Machine time, three arms**, dialogue plus primary judge: about 22 days
(200 tokens, a 100 tok/s seeker, 5 s per judge call) to about 63 (300 tokens,
30 tok/s, 10 s). None of it counts the pilot, the no-dialogue baselines
(1,000 administrations of 15 short items per arm; minutes to an hour in all,
PAP section 10) or reruns.

What the pilot has to measure, and where the numbers go:

- the mean `predicted_n` per message, per arm and for the seeker (`turns.jsonl`);
- the seeker's aggregate tok/s with its mentor on the same GPU: two servers
  sharing one GPU have never been benchmarked (gap audit F15);
- seconds per judge call at 8 slots, from `scores.jsonl` timestamps and the
  judge server's log;
- Olmo at np=4 or np=8 (open; `models/RUN_APPROACH.md`, "Olmo at np=4 or np=8").

**Note on the operating point** (parallelism review M2). A dyad is on one
server at a time, so `concurrency` 8 across a seeker and a mentor server gives
each about four active streams, not the eight the tok/s figures were measured
at. At np=4 the same CSV gives qwen3.6 107.2 (-8%), gpt-oss 82.5 (-1%) and Olmo
41.4 (0%). The review puts the dialogue-days error from this, from warm
prefill, from depth and from the survey requests at about 15 percent either
way, smaller than the turn length or the seeker's speed above.
